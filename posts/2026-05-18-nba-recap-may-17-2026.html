NBA Nightly Recap — May 17, 2026

Cavaliers 125, Pistons 94 — Cleveland Stomps Detroit Into Submission

If this was supposed to be Detroit's coming-out party, somebody forgot to send Cleveland the invitation. The Cavs rolled into Little Caesars Arena and put on a clinic, hanging 125 points on 51% shooting and turning what was billed as a measuring-stick night for the young Pistons into a 31-point laugher.

Who Decided It

- Donovan Mitchell ran the show with a tidy 26-6-8 on 46% shooting. No hero-ball needed — Spida picked his spots, kept Detroit's defense rotating, and was already on the bench for most of the fourth. The eight assists are the number that matters: when Mitchell is this engaged as a playmaker, this Cleveland offense becomes a five-headed problem. - Evan Mobley dropped a 21-12-6 line on 70% shooting that should be required film for anyone still asking whether he's a top-15 player. He punished Detroit's interior, created out of short rolls, and his fingerprints were everywhere — particularly during the second-quarter run that put this one on ice. - Sam Merrill and Jarrett Allen both poured in 23, with Merrill hitting from everywhere (70% from the floor) and Allen converting 57% as the lob-and-cleanup engine. When your fourth-best scorer goes for 23 on seven made buckets, the night is functionally over.

Tactical Turning Point

Watch the second quarter. Cleveland went small-ish around Mobley, started switching everything on the perimeter, and dared Cade Cunningham to beat them off the bounce. He couldn't — 13 points on 31% shooting, 36+ minutes of grinding — and once Detroit's offense bogged down into iso-ball, the Cavs feasted in transition. The Allen-Mobley duo simply walled off the rim, and Detroit's 35% team field goal night tells you everything: there was no Plan B.

The Bigger Picture

Detroit's two leads — Cunningham and Daniss Jenkins (17 on 33%) — combined to shoot well under 35%. Caris LeVert and Duncan Robinson provided efficient bench buckets, but in garbage time. Cleveland, meanwhile, looks like a team that has fully sorted out its postseason identity: Mitchell as offensive engine, Mobley as two-way fulcrum, Allen feasting on the glass, and a Merrill-led shooting cast that punishes any help. 11 turnovers, 51% from the floor, four players over 20 — that's a championship-caliber box score.

For Detroit, this is the kind of night that's instructive, not catastrophic. Cunningham's group is still climbing, and Cleveland is operating on a different plane right now.

---

What to Watch

- Can Cleveland's three-point shooting hold up against tougher defenses? 32% from deep tonight is fine when you're hitting 51% inside the arc, but the margin tightens against elite competition. - Cade Cunningham's response game. Back-to-back grinding nights from your franchise guard demand a bounce-back. Look for him to attack the rim earlier and trust his playmakers more. - Mobley's MVP-tier stretch continues. He's stacking 20-10-5 games like it's nothing — keep an eye on whether the leaguewide narrative finally catches up to the production.

More hoops tomorrow. Don't blink.
