NBA Nightly Recap — May 17, 2026

Cleveland Cavaliers stomp Detroit Pistons

If this was supposed to be a series, somebody forgot to tell Cleveland. The Cavs ran roughshod over Detroit on Sunday night behind a clinic in balanced offense — four players in the 20s, 51% from the field as a team, and just 11 turnovers. The Pistons, meanwhile, looked every bit like a team still figuring out what postseason basketball requires: 35% shooting, 31% from three, and 14 giveaways. The math wasn't subtle.

Who decided it:

- Donovan Mitchell was the conductor, finishing with 26 points, 6 rebounds, and 8 assists on 46% shooting in just 31 minutes. He didn't need a hero game — he needed an efficient one, and he delivered while spreading the wealth. - Evan Mobley posted the most complete line on the floor: 21 / 12 / 6 on 70% shooting. The point-forward elements of his game keep getting more comfortable, and he was the connective tissue every time Detroit tried to load up on Mitchell. - Sam Merrill was the dagger off the bench — 23 points on 70% shooting, doing what Merrill does: punishing closeouts and stretching the defense until it snapped. Jarrett Allen chipped in 23 and 7 on 57% for good measure. When your fourth-best scorer drops 21-12-6, the night is essentially over by halftime.

Tactical key moment: Cleveland's decision to keep Mobley as the offensive hub when Detroit doubled Mitchell. Every time Cade Cunningham and Ausar Thompson tried to swarm the ball at the point of attack, Mobley caught it at the elbow and either found Merrill on the weakside or attacked a tilted defense himself. It collapsed Detroit's coverage scheme and turned a tight first quarter into a layup line.

For Detroit, Cade Cunningham (13 pts, 5 ast on 31% shooting) never found rhythm, and the supporting cast couldn't bail him out — Daniss Jenkins led the bench with 17 but shot 33%, and there was no Duncan Robinson heat-check moment to swing momentum. The Pistons are a young team learning that playoff defenses simply don't give you the same daylight.

Standings/series implications: Cleveland once again looks like the team built for May basketball — defensively versatile with Mobley and Allen, deep enough that Merrill can swing a game, and led by a guard who's playing within himself. Detroit's future remains bright, but they'll need Cade to be a 25-7-7 guy on efficient volume for them to make these games competitive.
