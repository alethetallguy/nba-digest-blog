Cleveland Cavaliers vs. Detroit Pistons: Cavs Land the Haymaker

If this was a measuring stick, Cleveland just snapped the ruler in half. The Cavaliers shot a scorching 51% from the field and got four players over 20 in a comprehensive throttling of Detroit, who never got off the canvas — 35% shooting, 31% from three, 14 turnovers. When you give Donovan Mitchell a runway and a defense that can't string stops together, this is the math.

The Deciders

- Donovan Mitchell — 26/6/8 on 46% in just 31 minutes. The eight assists are the tell. Detroit threw bodies at him and Mitchell calmly tilted the floor, hunting Allen rolls and Merrill relocations rather than forcing the issue. This was Playmaker Spida, and that version is the scariest one. - Evan Mobley — 21/12/6 on 70% shooting. Six assists from your center is a defense-breaker, and Mobley's hub work in the high post against Detroit's drop coverage was the structural problem Cade and company couldn't solve. Add the 12 boards and a fistful of contests at the rim, and it's another reminder why he's the All-NBA cornerstone Cleveland built around. - Sam Merrill — 23 on 70% shooting off the bench. When your fifth option is shooting like that, the series math gets ugly fast. Merrill's gravity in dribble-handoffs with Mobley turned every closeout into a Cavs advantage.

Key Tactical Moment

Cleveland's decision to attack with the Mobley-Allen double-big lineup rather than spacing it out completely flipped the geometry of the game. Detroit countered with smaller, switch-heavy looks — and Mobley simply punished mismatches in the post while Allen (23 pts, 57% FG) ate every roll lane Cunningham vacated. Once Detroit went small to chase Merrill off screens, Cleveland just kept dumping it inside. Pick your poison; both bottles are loaded.

The Detroit Problem

Cade Cunningham, 13 points on 31% in 36+ minutes, looked gassed and pressed. Daniss Jenkins (17 pts, 33%) gave Detroit some juice off the bounce, but a 33% night from your second-leading scorer is a tough recipe. Duncan Robinson and Caris LeVert combined for 24 efficient points, but the stars-vs-stars exchange was a wipeout. Detroit's young core is ahead of schedule — but tonight was a lesson in what playoff-caliber half-court offense actually looks like.

Implications: Cleveland keeps stacking evidence that their offense scales in the postseason — six different scorers in double figures, ball movement that doesn't decay under pressure, and a defensive frontline that turns clean looks into contested ones. Detroit, meanwhile, has to find an answer for Mobley as a passer, because right now he's the screen-game cheat code unlocking the Cavs' entire halfcourt.
